Direct or indirect? The delegating scale

All of these phrases appear in the episode. Choose the level that fits your role and your relationship with the person.

  1. Most indirect“Would you mind helping me with…?”A peer you don’t know well
  2. Indirect“Could you give me a hand with…?”A friendly colleague at your level
  3. Semi-direct“I’m going to give this to you to do. Is that OK?”A clear request, softened with a check
  4. Direct“Could you follow this process?”Steps that must be done exactly
  5. Most direct“I’m making you responsible for…” / “I’m handing this over to you.”A manager assigning ownership

  • Phil says direct phrases sound like “what a manager would say”. Is direct language normal from colleagues in your culture?
  • Delegate one real task from your week to a partner, using an indirect phrase and a reassurance phrase.

Vocabulary

delegate
verb
to give part of your own work or a task to someone else to do.
A good manager knows which jobs to delegate to the team.
urgent
adjective
needing to be dealt with immediately.
The client's complaint felt urgent, so she dropped everything else.
independent
adjective
not needing help or extra explanation from anyone else.
That task is fairly independent, so I can just hand it over.
indirect
adjective
not stating something plainly or bluntly; softer and more roundabout.
He used fairly indirect language so the request didn't sound like an order.
direct
adjective
stating something plainly and clearly, without softening it.
As the deadline got closer, her instructions became more direct.
bossy
adjective
behaving as if you have the right to order other people around.
He tried to soften his tone so he didn't come across as bossy.
clarifying
verb (-ing)
making something clearer or easier to understand.
She spent five minutes clarifying exactly what the report should include.
template
noun
a ready-made example or pattern that shows how something should be done.
I sent him a template so his report would match the usual format.
awkward
adjective
uncomfortable or embarrassed in a social situation.
It felt a bit awkward correcting someone more senior than me.
concrete
adjective

Give them a concrete example instead of a general description.

Idioms & Set Phrases

a bit of a control freak
idiom

I'm a bit of a control freak, so handing over this project isn't easy.
the more the merrier
idiom
the more people who join in or help, the better the result will be.
We need extra hands on this launch, so the more the merrier.
a big thing

Trusting your team enough to let go of a task is a big thing.

Vocabulary in Use

See the Language in New Situations

There is no grammar focus in this episode. Instead, this tab puts the key delegating phrases into new workplace situations, so you can use them beyond the podcast.

01New Examples

delegate
  • As the new team lead, she had to learn to delegate rather than do everything herself.
  • You should delegate the data entry so you can focus on the strategy.
  • He finds it hard to delegate, even when he's clearly overloaded.
Would you mind helping me with...?
  • Would you mind helping me with the onboarding pack for the new starter?
  • Would you mind helping me with checking these figures before I send them off?
  • This softer opener works well with a colleague at the same level as you.
Could you give me a hand with...?
  • Could you give me a hand with the client presentation this afternoon?
  • Could you give me a hand with sorting the inbox while I'm on leave?
  • It sounds friendly rather than like a formal instruction.
I'm making you responsible for...
  • I'm making you responsible for all supplier queries going forward.
  • I'm making you responsible for the rota from next month onwards.
  • Use this when you genuinely have the authority to assign ownership.
I'm handing this over to you.
  • The account is in good shape now. I'm handing this over to you from Friday.
  • I've finished the first draft. I'm handing this over to you for the edit.
  • It signals a clean, complete transfer of a task or project.
Could you follow this process?
  • Could you follow this process every time a refund request comes in?
  • Could you follow this process for logging new leads from now on?
  • It's direct, so it suits instructions that must be followed exactly.
give me a shout
  • Give me a shout once the first batch is ready for review.
  • Give me a shout if the printer gives you any trouble again.
sort out
  • Can you sort out who's covering the desk while I'm at the conference?
  • We still need to sort out the schedule for next week's launch.
the more the merrier
  • We're short-staffed this week, so the more the merrier on the helpdesk.
  • Anyone who wants to join the planning call is welcome; the more the merrier.
template
  • I've built a template so the weekly report always looks the same.
  • Use the template in the shared folder before you send out the invoice.
