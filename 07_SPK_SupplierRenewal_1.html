Boardroom Briefing: Supplier Contract Renewal

Read the scenario, study the bid evaluation in Exhibit 1, then read the procurement memo. You will pitch one supplier to the board.

ScenarioYou work in procurement at Calder Appliances, the company from Lesson 5. Calder buys about 40,000 electronic control units a year for its ovens and dishwashers. Two years ago, Lena Marsh renewed the contract with Orion Supply at $18.40 a unit. That contract now ends in four months, and this time procurement has asked three suppliers for bids. The board will choose one at its next meeting.
Your taskYou are the Procurement Lead. In 3 to 4 minutes, present the three bids: say what the numbers are, explain so what (why they matter), then say now what: one clear recommendation, followed by questions.

Procurement Office · Briefing Memo to the Board

Our contract with Orion Supply comes up for renewal in four months. We have started early on purpose: testing a new supplier’s parts takes about ten weeks, so switching is still a real option, and every bidder knows it. All three suppliers responded. The headline is simple: no supplier is best at everything, so the decision depends on what we value most: price, speed or quality.

First, a look back. Two years ago, we renewed with Orion at $18.40 a unit, with a 3 percent limit on faulty units. Orion has kept that promise: its return rate is now 2.4 percent, down from 3.6. But it has not become faster. Stock still takes six weeks to arrive, and almost one delivery in ten is late.

Orion, our current vendor, sits in the middle on every measure. Northwell Components is the most expensive at $21.10 a unit, about $108,000 a year more than Orion at our volume, but it delivers in three weeks and has by far the best quality record, with fewer than one in a hundred units returned. Ferro Industrial is the cheapest, but its nine-week lead time and 4.8 percent return rate worry us: our quality team estimates that returns and delays could cancel out most of the savings. As Lena Marsh wrote after the last renewal, we do not need the lowest price; we need parts that work. Choosing Ferro on price alone would also weaken our leverage, because with stock arriving nine weeks after we order it, we would depend on them far more than they depend on us.

My recommendation is to go into the renewal talks with Northwell as our preferred partner. Quality and the three-week lead time should be non-negotiable; price is where we negotiate. If Northwell will not move on price, they could sweeten the deal with longer payment terms or a two-year warranty. This is not the moment to play hardball, however: we want a long relationship, not a one-off win.

Next steps: I am handing the final cost-of-ownership check over to finance, and I would like to delegate the contract wording to legal, so that we can sort out the details in parallel. We will ask Northwell and Orion to come to the table with their best and final offer by 14 November. Handled well, this renewal can be a win-win for both companies.

move

move
Show what you gain and what you pay.
“The trade-off is clear: we pay more per unit, but we get speed and quality.”

move

Rehearsal

The Three-Part Pitch: What, So What, Now What

A recommendation pitch needs only three moves. Learn them, hear them in a model, prepare for the board’s questions, then score yourself.

Say What the numbers are, explain So What (what they mean for this decision), then say Now What you want the board to do. A good pitch spends the most time on So What, because that is where you earn the recommendation.

01The Framework

PartTimePurposeSample phrase
1. Whatabout 60 secState the headline numbers plainly, before you interpret them.“Three suppliers bid: Orion at $18.40 a unit, Northwell at $21.10 and Ferro at $16.90.”
2. So Whatabout 90 secExplain the trade-off and why it matters for this decision.“The cheapest bid isn’t the cheapest choice once you count returns and delays.”
3. Now Whatabout 45 secMake one specific recommendation, name the next steps, open the floor.“I’d recommend Northwell, pending a quick cost check from finance.”

02Model Script

What
“Our contract with Orion ends in four months, and three suppliers have bid for the renewal. On price, Ferro is cheapest at $16.90 a unit, Orion is at $18.40 and Northwell is at $21.10. On speed and quality, the order is reversed: Northwell delivers in three weeks with a quality score of 8.6, and Ferro takes nine weeks with a score of 6.5.”
→ Numbers first, grouped by criterion, with no opinion yet.
So What
“The trade-off is clear. Northwell would cost us about $108,000 a year more than Orion, but fewer than one percent of its units come back. Ferro looks cheap, but almost five percent of its units are returned, and our quality team estimates that returns and delays could cancel out most of the savings. On our weighted score, Northwell comes first with 8.8.”
→ Names the trade-off, hedges the estimate (“estimates”, “could”), states the facts with confidence.
Now What
“So I’d recommend Northwell as our preferred partner. Quality and lead time are non-negotiable for us; price is where we will negotiate, and we’ll ask for their best and final offer by 14 November. I’m handing the cost-of-ownership check over to finance this week. I’m happy to take any questions.”
→ One recommendation, the next steps with owners and a date, then an open door.

03Anticipate the Board’s Questions

Boards push back. Prepare an answer for each question before you reveal the model.

The board asks…A strong answer
“Why not just negotiate Orion down again, like last time?”
Show answer“We can, and Orion’s bid is a good fallback. But two years ago we fixed Orion’s quality, not its speed. Even at a lower price, a six-week lead time and one late delivery in ten would still hold our factory back.”
“Why should we pay $108,000 more a year?”
Show answer“That’s a fair question. The extra cost buys us three-week deliveries and a return rate under one percent. Our quality team estimates that Ferro’s returns and delays alone could cost us most of the difference.”
“Why not just stay with Orion? It’s easier.”
Show answer“Orion is a reasonable middle option, but it doesn’t lead on anything. If we renew with Orion, we should at least use Northwell’s bid as leverage to improve their lead time.”
“What if Northwell won’t lower its price?”
Show answer“Then we ask them to sweeten the deal in other ways, for example longer payment terms or a two-year warranty. Price isn’t the only concession they can make.”
“How sure are you about the cost of Ferro’s returns?”
Show answer“It’s an estimate, not a certainty, which is why I’ve asked finance to run a full cost-of-ownership check before we sign.”
