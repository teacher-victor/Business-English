Chasing People

What happens when you ask someone to do something at work, and they don’t do it? It can be really frustrating at work when you are waiting for a reply to get the job done. In this episode of Office English, Pippa and Phil talk about how to chase people up, or remind them about a task.

Words and Phrases from the Episode

Every highlighted term from the transcript is here, with a simple meaning and a new example.

Vocabulary

respond
verb
To answer or reply to something that someone has said or sent.
I sent two emails, but nobody responded.
awkward
adjective
Uncomfortable and a little embarrassing, so you do not know what to say.
It feels awkward to ask the same person again and again.
polite
adjective
Showing good manners and respect for other people.
A polite email starts with a friendly greeting.
professional
adjective
Serious, correct and respectful in the way you behave at work.
Keep your tone professional, even when you feel angry.
deadline
noun
The day or time by which something must be finished.
The deadline for the report is Friday at five.
vague
adjective
Not clear or exact; without details.
His message was vague, so I did not know what to do.
firm
adjective
Strong and clear, with less room to say no. (Firmer = more firm.)
My second email was firmer than my first one.
urgency
noun
The need to act very quickly because something is important.
The red subject line shows the urgency of the problem.
consequences
noun
The things that happen as a result of an action or a decision.
There will be serious consequences if we miss the date.
threaten
verb
To say that you will cause trouble for someone if they do not do something.
Never threaten a colleague in an email.
senior
adjective
Higher in rank or position than other people in an organisation.
A senior manager must agree before we can start.
escalate
verb
To pass a problem to a more senior person because you cannot solve it alone.
If nobody answers, we will escalate the problem.
overlook
verb
To not notice something, or to forget to deal with it.
A small mistake can easily be overlooked.
cynical
adjective
Believing that people act only for selfish reasons, and not trusting them.
Adding ten names to an email can look cynical.

Idioms & Set Phrases

chase people up
phrasal verb
To remind people to do something that they have not done yet.
I have to chase people up when the forms are late.
over and over again
set phrase
Many times, one time after another.
I had to ask over and over again before he answered.
get the job done
set phrase
To finish a task that needs to be done.
We need everyone’s help to get the job done on time.
pick up the phone
idiom
To call someone instead of writing to them.
When emails fail, pick up the phone and talk.
follow up
phrasal verb
To contact someone again about something you said or sent earlier.
I will follow up with a short email after the call.
copy in
phrasal verb
To send an email to another person at the same time, so that they can read it too.
Please copy in my manager on your answer.
or else
set phrase
Used to say that something bad will happen if people do not do what you ask.
Finish it by noon, or else we lose the client.
get back to someone
phrasal verb
To reply to someone, usually after you have checked something.
Thanks for your message. I will get back to you tomorrow.

Useful Expressions

just wondering if you saw my last email
email phrase
A friendly first reminder that does not sound angry. (Wondering = thinking about it.)
Just wondering if you saw my last email about Monday’s meeting.
have you had a chance to...
email phrase
A polite question that shows you understand the person is busy.
Have you had a chance to look at the report yet?
if you are able to... that would be great
email phrase
A soft way to give a deadline. It sounds like a question, not an order.
If you are able to send the photos by Friday, that would be great.
I wanted to remind you about...
email phrase
A firmer second reminder. It is a statement, not a question.
I wanted to remind you about the form for the new project.
please could you finish this by...
email phrase
A polite request with a clear deadline.
Please could you finish this by four o’clock today?
otherwise
linking word
Used to say what will happen if the first thing does not happen.
Please sign today. Otherwise, we cannot start on Monday.
this is a final reminder about...
email phrase
A clear message that this is the last time you will ask.
This is a final reminder about the invoice for March.
this is your last chance...
email phrase
A strong phrase for the last email; use it only when it is really true.
This is your last chance to send us your ideas.
