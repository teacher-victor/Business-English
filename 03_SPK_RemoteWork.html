Internal Analytics Team · Briefing Memo

This quarter's survey covered 240 employees split across three working arrangements: fully remote, hybrid, and fully in-office. Average self-rated productivity was broadly similar across all three groups, though hybrid teams reported the highest score. The real story is in two other numbers: meeting hours and turnover.

Fully remote staff logged nearly ten hours of meetings a week, noticeably more than either hybrid or office staff, a pattern we think reflects a bottleneck in quick, informal decision-making once people aren't sitting near each other. Turnover tells a similar story: fully remote staff left at close to double the hybrid rate. If I'm honest, a few people on this team expected the opposite result going forward, since remote work is often sold as the arrangement employees want most.

One thing is clear from this data: productivity itself won't fall apart under any of these three models, but the hidden costs show up very differently depending on how a team is set up. At the end of the day, the board needs to decide not just where people work, but how well the current setup is actually sustainable long-term.

Previous Language, Ready to Present

First, a recap of the key terms from the previous Reading and Listening lessons, carried over into the memo above. Then, a phrase bank for presenting data formally and confidently.

Vocabulary Recap

turnover
noun
The rate at which employees leave an organization and are replaced.
High turnover makes it hard to keep a team's knowledge consistent.
noticeably
adverb
In a way that is easy to see or notice.
Attendance improved noticeably after the schedule change.
bottleneck
noun
A point in a process where progress is slowed or stopped.
Approvals from one manager became a bottleneck for the whole team.
sustainable
adjective
Able to continue over time without causing problems.
A plan that burns people out isn't a sustainable one.

Idioms & Set Phrases Recap

fall apart
idiom
To stop working or succeeding; to collapse.
The schedule started to fall apart once two people left.
show up
idiom
To arrive or appear somewhere; figuratively, to become visible in results or data.
The cost savings showed up in the very next quarter's numbers.

Useful Expressions Recap

if I'm honest
discourse marker
Introduces a frank or possibly uncomfortable opinion.
If I'm honest, the numbers surprised us too.
going forward
discourse marker
From this point in time into the future.
Going forward, we'll review this every quarter.
one thing is clear
discourse marker
Introduces a single confident conclusion after discussing uncertainty.
One thing is clear: the current model needs adjusting.
at the end of the day
discourse marker
Introduces the most important point, after considering everything.
At the end of the day, retention matters as much as output.

Presentation Phrase Bank

Sequencing
structure
Guide the board through your points in order.
"To start... Moving on to the second point... Finally..."
Highlighting a Trend
structure
Draw attention to the most important pattern in the data.
"What stands out here is..." / "If you look at the chart, you'll notice..."
Comparing
structure
Set two or more figures side by side.
"Compared to hybrid, remote shows noticeably higher turnover."
Hedging vs. Certainty
structure
Signal how confident you are in a claim.
"The data suggests..." (less certain) vs. "We can say with confidence that..." (more certain)
Recommending
structure
Move from describing data to proposing action.
"Based on this, I'd recommend..." / "Going forward, our suggestion is to..."
Inviting Questions & Closing
structure
End your presentation clearly and open the floor.
"To wrap up... I'm happy to take any questions."
Delivery Coach

Building Your Presentation

A strong boardroom presentation has four parts: Open the topic, walk through your Findings, draw out the Insight behind them, and give a clear Recommendation. Below is a model script using that structure, followed by a self-check and some practice.

01 · The Four-Part Framework

PartPurposeSample Opening Phrase
1. OpenState the topic and why the board is looking at it now"This quarter, we surveyed 240 employees across three working arrangements."
2. FindingsWalk through the key numbers in order, from least to most important"What stands out here is the gap in weekly meeting hours."
3. InsightExplain why the pattern matters, not just what it is"This points to slower decision-making once people aren't sitting near each other."
4. RecommendPropose a clear next step"Based on this, I'd recommend hybrid as our default arrangement going forward."

02 · Model Script

"This quarter, we surveyed 240 employees across three working arrangements: fully remote, hybrid, and fully office."
→ Open: states the topic and the scope of the data in one sentence.
"What stands out here is the gap in weekly meeting hours: fully remote staff logged almost ten hours, noticeably more than either other group."
→ Findings: names the single clearest pattern first, with the actual number.
"This points to a bottleneck in quick, informal decisions once people aren't sitting near each other, and it's likely connected to the higher turnover we're also seeing in that group."
→ Insight: connects two findings and explains the likely cause.
"Based on this, I'd recommend hybrid as our default arrangement going forward, with core hours to support faster decisions for fully remote teams. I'm happy to take any questions."
→ Recommend + Close: one clear action, then an open invitation for discussion.

03 · Self-Assessment Rubric

CriterionWhat to check
ClarityCould someone who missed the chart still follow your numbers?
StructureDid you clearly move through Open, Findings, Insight, and Recommend?
Vocabulary useDid you use at least three terms or phrases from the previous Reading or Listening lessons?
ConfidenceDid you hedge only where the data is genuinely uncertain, and sound sure everywhere else?
